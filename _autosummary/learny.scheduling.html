learny.scheduling#

Which item to ask next, and which options to show with it, from the response log.

A Strategy turns one learner’s responses into the next round of a choice game: a target item drawn from the candidates, and the options to show with it (the target plus distractors from learny.distractors). Per-item state (a Leitner box, an SM-2 ease, a memory stability) is rebuilt by replaying the responses, the same way learny.tracing rebuilds its estimates from the append-only log, so a strategy holds no state of its own and can be swapped at any time.

Strategies are chosen by key, which is what a settings screen offers:

>>> import random
>>> from learny.tracing import Response
>>> log = [Response('ada', 'tsu', 'wrong', at=0.0, meta={'chosen': 'shi'})]
>>> strategy = make_strategy('confusion_weighted', epsilon_explore=0.0)
>>> round_ = strategy.next_selection(log, ['tsu', 'shi', 'so', 'n'], now=0.0,
...                                  rng=random.Random(1))
>>> round_.target in round_.options, len(round_.options)
(True, 4)

The keys: uniform_random (the baseline), leitner (boxes), sm2 (SuperMemo 2, graded by response time), confusion_weighted (the default: the items the learner mixes up, against what they mix them up with), fsrs_lite (a memory-decay model) and weakest (items on the labels weakest() names). Spacing intervals are in days; in a single dense sitting little is ever due, so every scheduler falls back to an urgency ranking rather than stalling.

Picks the target, then distractors from what is known to be confusable.

Subclasses override pick_target(). Distractors come from the caller’s similarity, plus the learner’s own recent confusions when learner_confusions is on (the default for the strategies that model confusion; off for the baselines, which keep no memory of the learner’s mistakes).

confusion_boost: float = 2.0#

How much more a learner’s confusion weighs than a known similarity.

Items on the labels the learner model says are weakest; else the least likely right.

Replays the responses into a LearnerModel over items (whose labels say what each item exercises) and asks it for the weakest labels. Candidates carrying those labels are drawn in proportion to their weight on them; when no label is credibly weakest, in proportion to the chance of a wrong answer.
